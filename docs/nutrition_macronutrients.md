# Macronutrients: The Foundation of Human Nutrition

## What Are Macronutrients?
Macronutrients are nutrients required in large amounts to provide energy and support
bodily functions. There are three primary macronutrients: carbohydrates, proteins,
and fats. Each plays a distinct role in maintaining health.

## Carbohydrates
Carbohydrates are the body's primary energy source, providing 4 calories per gram.
They are classified as simple (sugars) or complex (starches and fibre).

- **Simple carbohydrates** are found in fruits, milk, and processed foods
- **Complex carbohydrates** are found in whole grains, legumes, and vegetables
- WHO recommends carbohydrates represent 45–75% of total daily energy intake
- Free sugars should be limited to less than 10% of total energy intake

## Proteins
Proteins provide 4 calories per gram and are essential for tissue repair, immune
function, and enzyme production. They are made up of amino acids, nine of which
are essential — meaning the body cannot produce them and they must come from food.

- Complete protein sources: meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy
- Incomplete protein sources: legumes, grains, nuts, seeds
- Recommended intake: 0.8g per kilogram of body weight per day for adults

## Fats
Fats provide 9 calories per gram — the most energy-dense macronutrient. They are
essential for hormone production, vitamin absorption, and cell membrane integrity.

- **Unsaturated fats** (healthy): found in olive oil, avocados, nuts, fish
- **Saturated fats** (limit): found in butter, red meat, coconut oil
- **Trans fats** (avoid): found in processed and fried foods
- WHO recommends total fat intake be less than 30% of total energy intake
- Saturated fats should be less than 10% of total energy intake
- Trans fats should be less than 1% of total energy intake

## Fibre
Dietary fibre is a complex carbohydrate that the body cannot digest. It slows
digestion, supports gut health, controls blood sugar, and reduces cholesterol.

- Recommended intake: at least 25g per day for adults
- Sources: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds

## Energy Balance
Total daily energy needs vary by age, sex, weight, and activity level. A balanced
diet provides adequate macronutrients without exceeding energy requirements.
Overconsumption of any macronutrient — particularly refined carbohydrates and
saturated fats — is associated with obesity, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease.
